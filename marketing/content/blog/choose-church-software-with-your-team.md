# Choose Church Software With Your Team, Not Just for Them

**Draft — review for audience and product accuracy before publication.**

New software can promise a cleaner process. The people who will use it can
tell you whether that process fits the work.

Before comparing features, invite a small cross-section of the team to
describe a real weekly task from beginning to end. Where does information
arrive? Who needs it next? Which handoffs are easy to miss? Listen for
workarounds as well as frustrations; a workaround may be protecting a
valuable practice that a new tool should preserve.

Turn those stories into a short list of needs. Separate essentials from
"maybe later." Include practical questions about roles, training, support,
accessibility, data handling, and how information can be exported if
circumstances change. A product demo should answer those questions, not only
show its most polished screen.

If possible, test one workflow with the people responsible for it. Notice how
much explanation it takes, what happens when something changes, and whether
the result is clear to the next person in line. A modest pilot often teaches
more than a long feature checklist.

The right choice is not necessarily the one with the most capabilities. It is
the one your team can use responsibly and consistently, while leaving more
attention for the people your church serves.
